\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Four axioms in conflict.}
Modern physics rests on four conceptual contradictions that are not
technical difficulties but conflicts of axioms: the superposition of
spacetime itself (quantum superposition is linear, while the
general-relativistic background is dynamical
\cite{einstein1915feldgleichungen}); the measurement problem (collapse
is not in the Schr\"odinger equation); the black-hole information
paradox (unitarity versus the horizon); and the vacuum-energy
catastrophe (some $10^{120}$ orders of magnitude).  Standard responses
keep the axioms and add entities --- extra dimensions, new fields, new
particles.  This series of papers follows the opposite strategy:
\emph{change the primitive}.  The first paper of the series
\cite{ProjectionPhysics,HibsPhysics} showed that a single entity,
\emph{flow}, carries the kinematics of physics: from a small set of
postulates --- vector light speed, three anchored directions, and the
counting of space-displacement strands --- it derives mass, information,
causality, the four forces, and the quantum relations, all as exact
identities formalized in Lean~4 with zero \texttt{sorry}.

\paragraph{The primitive is flow.}
In this worldview space itself is a flowing medium --- the same
geometric structure as the ``river model'' of black holes
\cite{hamilton2008river}, in which space flows inward through the
horizon like water through a waterfall.  A physical object is an
\emph{anchored pattern} of the flow: the photon is a pattern carried
completely by the flow, while mass is the anchoring of a pattern
against it.  From the helix geometry of strands, the framework recovers
the quantum relations that historically entered physics one by one ---
the Planck--Einstein quantization \cite{planck1978gesetz}, the de~Broglie
wave \cite{de1924recherches}, the Heisenberg uncertainty product
\cite{heisenberg1927anschaulichen} --- now as consequences of one
geometry rather than as postulates.

\paragraph{Charge: the most familiar and least understood quantum number.}
Among the quantum numbers, charge is special.  It is conserved; it comes
in integer multiples of $e$; and its accelerations radiate photons ---
the one process by which the classical world routinely manufactures
quanta.  Yet no axiom of the standard model explains why these facts
hold.  In flowing space the question acquires a geometric answer:
charge is the \emph{divergence} of the space-displacement flow.  A
positive charge is a source from which displacement diverges; a negative
charge is a sink into which it converges; the sign of the divergence is
the sign of the charge.  This paper's first addition turns that
statement into theorems, and follows where it leads: a sink is a
converging flow, convergence is flattening, and a fully flattened
flow is massless and neutral --- the photon.

\paragraph{The second addition: the half-phase geometry.}
Where does the value $1/2$ itself come from?  Spin~$1/2$ is the
simplest fermionic quantum number, yet in standard physics it is
entered by hand --- Dirac wrote it into the equation
\cite{dirac1928quantum}, Pauli read it off the exclusion principle
\cite{pauli1925zusammenhang}, but nobody has derived it from geometry.
The present work locates it: the Riemann--Hilbert envelope of the
framework \cite{RiemannHIBS} carries the reflection $s\mapsto 1-s$,
whose fixed point is necessarily $1/2$; in the logarithmic coordinate
$w=e^s$ that fixed point is $\ln(\sqrt e)=1/2$, and the square root is
recognized as the half-step operator of the multiplicative group ---
in phase space, the half-angle phase $\exp(i\theta/2)$ whose closure
factor is $-1$.

\paragraph{One fact, two faces.}
The two additions are independent in content but share one theorem-level
fact: \emph{a quadratic quantity is reduced by a square factor}.  For
spin, the half-angle phase returns to itself with factor
$-1=(-1)^2$-free of any square: the half step of the variable
$\theta$ is the square root in logarithmic coordinates, and the
fermionic witness is $\exp(i\theta/2)$.  For charge, the fluctuation
energy of a converging flow is reduced by $(1-\lambda)^2$ per
contraction step; the sink is a self-flattening structure, and a sink
at full strength ($\lambda\to 1$) brings the fluctuation to zero, mass
to zero, and charge to neutrality --- the photon.

Section~II recalls the three postulates needed here.  Section~III

\section{Postulates of the flowing-space framework}
\label{sec:postulates}

We recall the postulates needed here; full derivations are in
Ref.~\cite{ProjectionPhysics}.  Each postulate is stated, then read
physically, so that the geometry of Secs.~III--VII remains anchored.

\begin{postulate}[Vector light speed]
\label{post:sls}
Space itself flows at the vector speed $C$ with $|C|=c$; the
direction of $C$ is changeable.
\end{postulate}

The river model of black holes \cite{hamilton2008river} is the same
picture: the medium moves, and what we call the speed of light is the
speed of the medium.  The direction being changeable is what allows
flows to curl, converge, and diverge --- the degrees of freedom used
throughout this paper.

\begin{postulate}[Three anchored directions]
\label{post:three}
Space has three independent orthogonal directions; a physical object
is an anchored pattern of the flow, with anchor strength
$s\ge 0$.  The anchored mass is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:anchor}
m^2_{\rm eff}\;=\;s^2(1-\mu)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $\mu\in[0,1]$ is the antigravity strength
(flattening of the flow gradient); $\mu=1$ gives $m_{\rm eff}=0$
(MassCancellation AMC1).
\end{postulate}

Mass is anchoring: the more strongly a pattern grips the flow, the
more massive it is --- and the flatter the flow gradient, the less
there is to grip.  The quantity $\mu$ measures how far the flattening
has gone; $\mu=1$ is the completely flat flow, in which nothing can be
anchored and everything is carried.

\begin{postulate}[Four forces as Leibniz channels]
\label{post:four}
The flow momentum is $P=m(C-v)$; its time derivative decomposes into
four channels (GQF2):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:four}
\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[m(C-v)\bigr]
\;=\;\underbrace{\dot m\,C}_{\text{electric}}
+\underbrace{m\,\dot C}_{\text{nuclear}}
-\underbrace{\dot m\,v}_{\text{magnetic}}
-\underbrace{m\,\dot v}_{\text{gravity}} .
\end{equation}
\end{postulate}

Force is change of flow momentum, and the Leibniz rule splits that
change into four named channels: change of mass while the medium flows
(electric), change of the medium itself (nuclear), change of mass while
the body moves (magnetic), and change of the body's own motion
(gravity).  The channel activated by a varying medium, $m\,\dot C$, is
the one this paper will need.

\section{Charge as flow divergence}
\label{sec:charge}

Charge is the divergence of the space-displacement flow
(GQF4; VibrationChargeFlow CF1--CF5).  The picture to hold on to is a
whirlpool: a sink drains the surrounding surface toward itself, a
source pushes it outward --- the same medium, two opposite signs of
convergence.  This is also the oldest dynamical picture of the field
itself: Maxwell built electrodynamics on the stresses of a medium
\cite{maxwell1996dynamical}, and here the medium is space.

\begin{theorem}[Source and sink]
\label{def:srcsink}
For a local divergence scalar $\rho$:
\begin{align}
\text{PositiveSource}(\rho) &:\Longleftrightarrow 0<\rho ,\\
\text{NegativeSink}(\rho)   &:\Longleftrightarrow \rho<0 .
\end{align}
Positive charge is a source (diverging displacement), negative charge
a sink (converging displacement); both obey the right-handed helical
motion of the flow, the sign of the divergence distinguishing them.
\end{theorem}

The helical motion is shared --- a source and a sink spiral the same
way; only the sign of $\rho$ tells them apart.  Charge is not a new
substance added to the flow; it is a local property of the flow
itself, the divergence of its displacement.

\begin{theorem}[Source--sink exclusivity; CF1--CF4]
\label{thm:srcsink}
(i)~A source and a sink cannot hold simultaneously.  (ii)~Charge
conjugation is the sign flip: $0<\rho \iff \rho<0$ after
$\rho\mapsto-\rho$ (source and sink are exchanged).  (iii)~Zero
divergence is neutral: neither source nor sink.  (iv)~The magnitude is
preserved under the flip: $|{-}\rho|=|\rho|$.
\end{theorem}

The four algebraic facts are the whole charge algebra in miniature:
exclusivity (no point is both), conjugation (flipping the sign swaps
the two), neutrality (zero divergence carries no charge), and magnitude
(the amount of charge is untouched by conjugation).  All four are
theorems of the divergence structure, not assumptions about a quantum
number.

The continuity side --- the coupled extraction of the charge $e$, the
divergence dynamics in 3D, and charge normalization --- remains open
(honestly registered in the repository).  In particular, this paper
does not yet derive the value of $e$ or the discrete charge spectrum;
it derives the geometric content of the sign.

\section{Accelerated charge implies the field varies; the nuclear
channel activates}
\label{sec:accel}

The story now turns from statics to dynamics.  If charge is the
divergence of the flow, an accelerated charge is a time-varying
divergence: a source whose strength changes with time.  What does that
do to the space field $C$?  The sourced wave equation --- the
framework's equation of motion for the medium, in the same family as
light propagation through a dielectric \cite{gordon1923lichtfortpflanzung}
--- answers in one stroke.

\begin{theorem}[CR1; time-varying source forces a varying field]
\label{thm:cr1}
In the sourced wave equation
$\partial_t^2 C=c^2\partial_x^2 C+J$ (MS5), a static field implies a
static source: if $\partial_t C\equiv 0$, then $J(t{+}1)=J(t)$ for all
$t$.  Hence a time-varying source (an accelerated charge) forbids a
static space field.
\end{theorem}

The contrapositive is the physical content: a field that does not
change can only be fed by a source that does not change.  Shake the
charge, and the medium must shake with it --- the field varies.

\begin{theorem}[CR2; the nuclear channel activates]
\label{thm:cr2}
With $m\neq 0$ and $\dot C\neq 0$, the nuclear channel of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:four}) is non-zero: $m\,\dot C\neq 0$.
\end{theorem}

A varying medium means $\dot C\neq 0$, and any anchored body in it
feels the nuclear channel of the Leibniz decomposition: the force that
comes from the medium changing underneath the body, not from the body
changing its own motion.

The missing link between the nuclear channel and the antigravity
parameter $\mu$ is not invented here: the framework already contains
its own $\mu$-bridge.  The bridge is the flattening progress of the
flow --- how much of the fluctuation energy has been removed --- and it
feeds $\mu$ directly.

\begin{theorem}[CR3; one flattening gives $\mu=1$]
\label{thm:cr3}
Let $Q_A(v)=\sum_{i\in A}(v_i-\bar v_A)^2$ be the fluctuation energy,
and $\eta=1-Q_A(v)/Q_A(v_0)$ the flattening progress (MuFieldCoupling
TD11).  The $\mu$ update is $\mu'=\mu+\eta(1-\mu)$
(PlasmaDynamics muStep).  Flattening once gives
$\mu'=\mu+1\cdot(1-\mu)=1$.
\end{theorem}

The update rule says: $\mu$ gains, per step, a fraction $\eta$ of what
remains between it and $1$.  A single complete flattening ($\eta=1$)
therefore lands exactly on $\mu=1$ --- not asymptotically, in one step.

\begin{theorem}[CR4; $\mu=1$ is the photon endpoint]
\label{thm:cr4}
At $\mu=1$, the anchored mass vanishes
(Eq.~(\ref{eq:anchor}) with $1-\mu=0$) and the divergence is neutral
(CF3).  The object is massless and chargeless --- the photon.
\end{theorem}

Two conditions for a photon fall out of the same endpoint: the flat
flow can hold no anchor (mass zero), and the flat flow has no
divergence (charge zero).  The photon of the heuristic quanta of 1905
\cite{einstein1905erzeugung} is here the completely unanchored,
divergence-free pattern --- the pattern carried entirely by the medium.

\begin{theorem}[CR5; closure of the chain]
\label{thm:cr5}
One flattening applied to any region $A$ (any anchored substance $s$)
yields a photon: mass zero and neutrality.
\end{theorem}

The closure is universal: no property of the particular substance
enters.  Whatever is anchored, one flattening of its region
transmutes it.  The chain is conditional --- accelerated charge
$\Rightarrow$ varying field $\Rightarrow$ nuclear channel
$\Rightarrow$ flattening $\Rightarrow$ $\mu=1$ $\Rightarrow$ photon ---
and its only unproved link is the second-input gap stated in Sec.~IX:
who applies the flattening.

\section{Convergence is flattening: sink contraction}
\label{sec:sink}

Why is the \emph{negative} charge the natural antigravity carrier?
The geometric answer: a sink is a converging flow, and convergence is
flattening.  Picture the whirlpool again: water that converges on the
drain has, by the time it arrives, lost its individual differences ---
the surface is level at the center.  The same contraction, applied to
the space flow, removes the fluctuations that constitute internal
structure.  In miniature, this is what a warp contraction does to the
space ahead of a traveler \cite{alcubierre1994warp} --- a contraction
of the medium itself, not a motion through it; we use the picture only
as an image.

\begin{theorem}[Sink contraction; CR9]
\label{def:sinkcontract}
For a region $A$, a field $v:A\to\mathbb R$, and $\lambda\in[0,1]$,
\[
\text{sinkContract}(A,v,\lambda)(i)
\;=\;\begin{cases}
(1-\lambda)\,v_i+\lambda\,\bar v_A & i\in A,\\
v_i & i\notin A .
\end{cases}
\]
Each point of $A$ is pulled toward the region mean by a fraction
$\lambda$ --- the discrete image of ``the negative charge absorbs the
surrounding flow toward itself.''
\end{theorem}

The parameter $\lambda$ is the strength of the sink per step: $\lambda$
of the way toward the mean at every application.  The operator is
entirely local and needs no information beyond the current field ---
the sink acts by itself, which is why its effect will later be called
self-flattening.

\begin{theorem}[CR9; square decay of fluctuation]
\label{thm:cr9}
The contraction preserves the region mean and scales the fluctuation
energy by $(1-\lambda)^2$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cr9}
Q_A\bigl(\text{sinkContract}(A,v,\lambda)\bigr)
\;=\;(1-\lambda)^2\,Q_A(v).
\end{equation}
For $0<\lambda<1$ the decay is monotone and exact; $\lambda=1$ is a
single flattening (fluctuation to zero).  The sink is a
self-flattening operator family.
\end{theorem}

The fluctuation energy --- the very quantity that measures how much
internal structure the region carries --- dies as $(1-\lambda)^2$,
quadratically, not linearly.  Two facts make the theorem exact rather
than approximate: the mean is preserved (so the contraction has no
secular drift), and the scaling is a square (so even a weak sink
eventually empties the region of fluctuation).

\begin{theorem}[CR10; strict attraction pointwise]
\label{thm:cr10}
For $0<\lambda<1$ and $v_i\neq\bar v_A$,
\[
\bigl|\text{sinkContract}(A,v,\lambda)(i)-\bar v_A\bigr|
\;<\;\bigl|v_i-\bar v_A\bigr| ,
\]
each point is strictly attracted to the mean --- ``the sink pulls the
surrounding flow to itself,'' pointwise.
\end{theorem}

Every point moves strictly closer on every step; nothing oscillates,
nothing is left behind.  The attraction is uniform in the sense that
the contraction factor applies to every deviation, which is what makes
the numerical approach (Sec.~VIII) monotone.

The energy side of the sink is already present in the framework:
information sinking into the absorbing branch $iR$ releases
$\varphi(R)-\varphi(iR)=\delta+\varepsilon>0$ (HiddenQFT HQ9a), so a
converging flow carries its own flattening power as a candidate
(CR8).  This is a semantic correspondence (interface), not a theorem
of the divergence dynamics --- a distinction that Sec.~IX keeps
explicit.

\section{The half-phase geometry: spin~$1/2$ from the critical sheet}
\label{sec:halfphase}

The second geometric addition concerns the value $1/2$ itself.  The
guide is a logarithmic ruler: a full step multiplies by $e$; the half
step multiplies by $\sqrt e$.  A mirror at the halfway point fixes the
half: the reflection $s\mapsto 1-s$ keeps only $1/2$ fixed, and in the
logarithmic coordinate the mirror is the inversion $w\mapsto e/w$,
whose fixed points lie on the circle $|w|=\sqrt e$.  The identity
$\ln(\sqrt e)=1/2$ is then not a curiosity of the logarithm but the
statement that the fixed point of the reflection must sit at the
halfway mark of the unit interval.

\subsection{The inversion fixed point}

The Riemann--Hilbert envelope of the framework
\cite{RiemannHIBS} carries the reflection $s\mapsto 1-s$; in the
logarithmic coordinate $w=e^s$ this is the inversion $w\mapsto e/w$.
Its fixed set is the circle $|w|=\sqrt e$ (envelope inversion fixed
circle).  Taking logarithms:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:half}
\ln(\sqrt e)\;=\;\tfrac12\ln e\;=\;\tfrac12 .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[CS2; inversion fixed iff log half]
\label{thm:cs2}
For $r>0$,
\[
r=e/r \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \ln r=\tfrac12 .
\]
The fixed point of the reflection must sit at $1/2$ in the
logarithmic coordinate --- because $\ln e=1$ and the fixed point of
$s\mapsto 1-s$ is $1/2$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem says the two facts are the same fact: being fixed under
inversion (in the multiplicative picture) and being halfway (in the
logarithmic picture).  The value $1/2$ is forced by the geometry of a
reflection, not chosen.

\subsection{The half-angle phase is fermionic}

The square root is the half-step operator of the multiplicative group;
in the phase space of a vibration, the same half-step is the half-angle
phase.  Walk the unit circle in half steps: after one turn the pointer
has advanced by only half a cycle --- and a half cycle is a sign flip.
The sign flip is the entire physical content of fermionic statistics at
the level of phases.

\begin{theorem}[CS4; half-angle phase has closure factor $-1$]
\label{thm:cs4}
$\theta\mapsto\exp(i\theta/2)$ satisfies
\[
f(\theta+2\pi)\;=\;-1\cdot f(\theta),
\]
i.e.\ it is a fermionic closure witness (VibrationStatistics
ClosureFactor with $\sigma=-1$): $2\pi$ changes sign, $4\pi$
restores.  The half-angle phase is the explicit witness of
spin~$1/2$ in the phase space of a vibration.
\end{theorem}

This is the phase-geometric half of the spin--statistics story: a
spinor needs $4\pi$ to return to itself, and the half-angle phase is
why.  Dirac's equation \cite{dirac1928quantum} encodes the same fact
in $\gamma$-matrices; here it appears as the closure factor of the
half-step map itself.  Sec.~IX states precisely what this theorem does
and does not establish.

\subsection{Energy and mass}

With $E\propto m^2$ (mass--energy quadrature), logarithms are
linear: $\ln E=2\ln m$.  At the critical sheet, $\ln(\sqrt e)=1/2$
means $m=\sqrt e$, $E=e$; the factor $2$ in $\ln E=2\ln m$ is the
inverse of the spin half-step --- energy takes two half-steps to
complete one cycle of $m$.

\begin{theorem}[CS5; logarithmic $2{:}1$]
\label{thm:cs5}
$\ln e=2\ln(\sqrt e)$.
\end{theorem}

The quadrature $E\propto m^2$ and the half-step $\ln(\sqrt e)=1/2$
conspire: the factor $2$ of the logarithm is the reciprocal of the
half-step, so energy advances in mass at twice the rate that mass
advances in itself --- two half-steps per cycle, the fermionic
signature read again in the energy--mass plane.

\section{Device application: two-flow-ring optimization}
\label{sec:device}

\label{eq:etasink}
\eta_{\text{sink}}(\lambda)=2\lambda-\lambda^2 ,

the fraction of fluctuation removed by one contraction, added to the
external RMF gain and decaying with the residual

geometry locates spin~$1/2$: $\ln(\sqrt e)=1/2$ is the fixed point of
the envelope reflection in the logarithmic coordinate, and the
half-angle phase is the explicit fermionic closure witness.
(v)~All statements are formalized in Lean~4 with zero

\paragraph{Not claimed.}
(i)~The bridge ``nuclear channel $\Rightarrow$ flattening action''

The boundary is maintained throughout: what is claimed is certified
(theorem plus proof, machine-checked); what is not yet a theorem is
labeled as such in this section, never upgraded in the body.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Two geometric structures extend the flowing-space framework.  The
divergence structure of charge makes the sink a self-flattening
operator, closing --- conditionally --- the chain from accelerated
charge to the photon and giving the two-flow-ring device a
self-flattening gain that shortens the approach to the working window
by a third.  The half-phase geometry locates spin~$1/2$ at the
envelope-reflection fixed point $\ln(\sqrt e)=1/2$, with the
half-angle phase as its explicit fermionic witness.  Both additions
are theorem-exact where they are exact, and honestly open where the
inputs remain: $\lambda$, $\delta$, $\varepsilon$, and the continuum
3D correspondence.

The open items assemble into a short list of problems in the spirit of
Hilbert's program \cite{hilbert1900mathematische}: derive the
flattening power from the divergence dynamics; derive $\lambda$ and
the sink energy scale; formalize the continuum 3D
divergence-to-functional correspondence; and complete the
spin--statistics theorem in field-operator language.  Each is a
well-posed question that this paper leaves with the boundary stated
rather than blurred --- the road is followed precisely, and the
mountain is climbed only a little, just enough to be sure it is a
mountain.
